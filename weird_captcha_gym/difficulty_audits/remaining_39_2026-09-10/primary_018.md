# Cloudpost Circuit — primary assessment

Decision: leave L4/Full unchanged and provisional; retain the relative control ladder with an input-domain qualification and incomplete L4/L5 browser evidence. The parent read the complete core implementation during the earlier capability audit and reread the complete generator, browser JavaScript, grader and solver for these difficulty checks. No measured absolute calibration is available.

More seals alone do not justify higher levels. Smaller contact spheres, larger required heading/altitude excursions, closer spacing, faster advance and slower heading response change the active control problem. Additional scenery is not collision geometry. Neither three-dimensional rendering nor two-axis control alone proves a level above Gyroscopic Tilt Board.

The reviewer correctly identifies a grading-domain gap: Simplified buttons apply bounded increments of 0.12 yaw or 0.10 pitch, while the grader accepts arbitrary finite control vectors marked `trim_button`. The browser really restricts the controls, and opposite-mode source markers are rejected. This is an exported-transcript contract issue, not a screenshot-only exploit or a reason to remove inspectable state from the required static browser architecture.

The six original L1–L3 browser solves passed. L4/L5 did not: both Full controllers failed to reach the first seal; both Simplified cases reached the 300-second harness deadline. The first archived attempts show corridor failure, not a successful browser solution rejected by the grader. The unchanged solver can continue pursuing its old target after failure creates a new challenge; a late harness timeout is not the time at which the original flight failed.

[Start-delay checks](cloudpost_start_delay_checks.json) separate one cause from reachability at initialization. Exact-pose, ideal continuous-control replays starting at tick zero pass the independent grader for all 15 generated worlds. Only 10 pass when the same controller waits 16 neutral ticks. In the original L5 seed-1 runs, the first input arrives after 0.64s/0.68s of live flight. At that point the minimum distance from the first target to the future reachable x-z half-plane is 21.57/23.88 units, larger than its 10-unit contact radius. The first seal is already unreachable even before accounting for turn ramp or button latency. The analogous L4 bound does not establish impossibility; controller drift and startup delay remain relevant but are not fully separated there.

These results show why initialization, observation capture and action-delay schedules must be specified before calibration. They do not authorize silently pausing the task, moving its seals, increasing tolerances, or counting an ideal private-state replay as a browser pass. L4/L5 browser and Simplified feasibility evidence remain incomplete. No real-time capability reclassification is made here.
