# Crackglaze Crossing — primary assessment

Decision: revise the Full click/grader geometry and qualify the hidden-stage descriptions; retain the existing L3/Full baseline provisionally. The parent read the complete controls, generator, browser JavaScript/CSS, grader, task verifier, solver and task/environment specifications. The initial/final rendered states of the separate edge-click reproduction were visually inspected.

The Full geometry mismatch is confirmed by actual browser interaction, not just by comparing formulas. Browser `pointInBoard` normalizes the click against the transformed board bounding box, but the grader treats cells as an unpadded equal grid. The CSS adds 18px padding, gaps and perspective. At D3/Full, seed 1, a real click at `(1281.1934, 279.9566)` hit visible tile `r0c5` and moved the player there. Its recorded normalized x was `0.8235543`; the grader's left boundary, including tolerance, was `0.8333333 - 0.008 = 0.8253333`. On completion of the otherwise unchanged passing route, the server rejected `event 5 misses the visible destination tile` and regenerated the challenge. See `browser_checks/024_crackglaze_crossing_d3_full_seed1_solve_crack_tile_edge_diagnostic.json` and its archived failed attempt. The original ten center-click/proxy-button solves remain passing records; this diagnostic is separate.

This is a task defect affecting valid Full clicks, not a difficulty measurement, an unsolvable-world claim, or a reason to weaken the grader blindly. Rendering, input coordinates and replay geometry need one consistent mapping in a separately authorized fix. Source: browser lines 15–21 and 160–184, CSS lines 119–132, grader lines 39–52 and 194–201.

The gallery selector hides the crack strokes but leaves stage-specific brightness, saturation, displacement and shattered-hole appearance active. Therefore the claim that stages cannot be seen is too strong. The degree to which those remaining cues identify age has not been measured; do not count every gallery tile as fully hidden state. The source effect follows CSS stage selectors around lines 183–232.

The route and lifetime construction is substantive: it rejects several selected geometry-only routes and includes a same-geometry/different-lifetime counterfactual with incompatible certified routes. This is stronger evidence than merely adding lanterns or moves. It still does not prove that every winning policy must revisit every selected gallery tile, nor establish strict adjacent-level ordering on all seeds. `minimum_policy_failures` is a generator filter over a bounded policy set, not an action quota or an absolute difficulty score. Longer fuses at some upper levels also increase recovery margins while other demands rise.

No puzzle sources or labels were changed. All source references refer to the frozen revision under `weird_captcha_gym/`.
